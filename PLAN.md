# AI Is the Tool. You're Still the Expert.

Approved working plan. About 39 minutes of speaking, plus 6 minutes for Q&A.
Every tip follows: THE TIP → WHY IT MATTERS → REAL EXAMPLE → TRY THIS.
Slides can be combined for pacing, but teaching content is not cut to hit a count.

## Design rules

- Palette from the Master Chef storyboard: espresso, cream, sand, tomato, mustard, teal.
- Type: Inter Tight for headlines, Inter for text. Local font files for offline use.
- Alternate light and dark slides.
- One idea per slide. Explanations live in presenter notes.
- Cohesion comes from the design system, not a repeated character.
- The chef and restaurant analogy appears ONLY in Tip 4.
- Speech bubbles are used mainly in the introduction and Tip 1.
- Visual approach by section:
  - Communication: characters, bubbles, arrows, app icons
  - Prompting (Tip 1): an unfolding conversation
  - Critical thinking (Tip 2): before and after, revealed blind spots
  - Workflow (Tip 3): diagrams and connected steps
  - Skills (Tip 4): owner, chef, order ticket, recipe
  - Creative uses (Tip 5): unexpected comparisons and reveals
  - Closing: bold typography
- Reading level: grades 6 to 8. AP style with the Oxford comma.
- No AI-generated images. Original SVG art and generic icons only, no brand logos.
- Reduced-motion support. Nothing advances on its own.

## Accuracy checklist

- Skills give more consistency, not identical results, and they don't run on their own.
- A reusable prompt or instruction file is not the same as a platform-specific skill.
- Skills are not autonomous agents.
- Slash codes are custom shortcuts, not built-in commands. Test "/" in each app before the talk.
- Most chats use one model. Only some tools coordinate multiple agents.
- Connectors only reach apps you connect and allow.
- Tom Wujec's TED Talk title: confirm the exact title and link before adding to notes.
- Remind people to verify important output and protect customer and private data.
