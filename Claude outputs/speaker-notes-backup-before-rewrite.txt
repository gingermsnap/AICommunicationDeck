===== Slide 9 (tip-2) =====
THE TIP. This is tip 2 of 5.

Tip 1 was about how you talk to AI. Tip 2 is about how you question what AI tells you.

AUDIENCE MOMENT (20 seconds)
Question: "Raise your hand if AI has ever agreed with you a little too fast."
Takeaway: AI is built to be helpful, so it tends to agree with you. Agreeing isn't the same as being right.

Don't just take its word for it. Push back. And let AI push back, too.

Good AI conversations involve questioning, feedback, and iteration. The first answer isn't always the best answer. AI can sound confident and still be wrong.

One of my favorite ways to use AI is to red-team my own thinking. I don't just want AI to agree with me. I want it to tell me what I'm missing.

[CLICK 1] "I don't think you understood what I am saying."

[CLICK 2] "Let's take another look." It doesn't get offended. It's happy to look again. You just have to ask.

Transition: First, why this matters. There's research on this.

===== Slide 10 (tip-2-agree) =====
THE RESEARCH. This is why pushing back matters. (The number counts up on its own when the slide opens. No click needed.)

A Stanford team led by Myra Cheng tested 11 leading AI models. AI affirmed users' actions 49% more often than humans did. Even when the person was describing something deceptive or harmful.

They also found people liked the agreeable AI more and trusted it more. That's the trap. It feels good to be agreed with.

So the agreement isn't proof you're right. Let me show you what that looks like with a real business question.

Source: Myra Cheng, Cinoo Lee, Pranav Khadpe, Sunny Yu, Dyllan Han, and Dan Jurafsky, "Sycophantic AI decreases prosocial intentions and promotes dependence," Science 391, no. 6792 (March 26, 2026). DOI: 10.1126/science.aec8352

===== Slide 12 (tip-2-example) =====
THE CONVERSATION. This is an illustrative scenario, not a real analysis.

Here I am, talking with AI about my business.

[CLICK 1] AI recommends: "Raise your prices by 10%. You'll increase revenue without needing more customers." Sounds confident. Sounds reasonable.

[CLICK 2] I don't argue. I just type one word: /redteam.

[CLICK 3] Watch what happens. AI takes another look. It says my prices are already competitive in this example. And the biggest thing it missed: it never asked why I wanted more revenue. Am I trying to attract more customers, improve profit margins, or increase repeat business? That changes the recommendation.

One shortcut changed the direction of the whole conversation. It went from an answer to a better question.

Important: asking AI to check itself doesn't guarantee it's right. It exposes assumptions so you can think better. You still check the facts, and you still make the call.

===== Slide 16 (tip-3-maptry) =====

===== Slide 19 (tip-3-training) =====
EMPLOYEE TRAINING. Here's a recipe almost every small business can use.

A lot of us have years of experience training employees. But very little of it is written down. It lives in your head.

The recipe: Employee Training. A repeatable process for creating training guides.

[CLICK 1] The ingredients. Your existing procedures and notes, your business knowledge and experience, who you're training, and what they need to learn or do.

[CLICK 2] The instructions. Organize the information into a step-by-step training guide. Explain each step in plain language. Include practical examples. Create a checklist and a short quiz. And flag anything that's missing, then give me a draft to review.

[CLICK 3] And my way: "Keep it friendly. Assume they're brand new. Use our business's tone and examples. Don't invent company policies!" That last one matters. If AI doesn't know your policy, you want it to ask, not make one up.

[CLICK 4] The finished dish: a training guide, a checklist, and a quiz, ready for your review.

You review it for accuracy before any employee sees it. You're still the expert.

===== Slide 21 (tip-4-gov2) =====
THE RECIPE AND THE RESULT.

[CLICK 1] The recipe. My requirements, turned into reusable instructions. That's Tip 4.

[CLICK 2] The ingredients. The source information and who it's for. The goal and the format. And the facts, dates, links, and official wording that have to be there.

===== Slide 22 (tip-4-skill) =====
ADVANCED TIP. Turn your recipe into a skill.

Quick recap of where we've been. Your workflow is how you do the work. Your recipe is the instructions AI can follow. A skill is those instructions saved so you can use them again.

Once you've tested and refined your recipe, you can save it to use again. Some AI tools let you package these instructions as a reusable skill.

[CLICK 1] Here's the prompt: "Help me turn this tested recipe into a reusable AI skill. Interview me one question at a time to understand my instructions, preferences, and what must never change." (Give people a second to take a photo.)

You don't need to know how to build one yourself. AI can walk you through it.

[CLICK 2] Your recipe becomes a skill.

[CLICK 3] To be clear: a skill is a saved set of instructions. It's not an AI that goes off and works on its own. And not every AI tool supports skills, so you may need to save the instructions somewhere and paste them in. That still works.

Test it. Refine it. Reuse it.
